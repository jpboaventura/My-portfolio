Quick Disconnect for Liquid Rocket

Apr. 2024 - Present | University of Toronto – UTAT Rocketry Division

Project Description

This project involves designing a new Quick Disconnect (QD) system for UTAT’s first liquid rocket. This QD is used to start the engine pneumatically, and must hence be disconnected within 0.1 s. As a result, this QD requires both an active and passive mechanism of disconnection to ensure the line is disconnected before the rocket leaves the launch rail. This project exposed me to many aspects of the design cycle, from idea conception, to design of prototypes (using CAD and 3D printing) and testing campaigns and subsequent iteration.

Role

As co-lead of the Structures subsystem, I led the testing campaign for the pneumatic engine QD. The goal was to determine the force of disconnection and optimal angle of disconnection, with the testing rig and images from testing shown above. These were important considerations because a passive disconnection mechanism was crucial for this QD due to the fast disconnection time required. This was chosen to be a rope attached to the female end of the QD and would hence require the QD to be angled out of the rocket. From an airframe and aerodynamics perspective, the ideal angle would be 90 degrees from the vertical (i.e., sticking straight out of the rocket) as the hole size would be minimized (preserving airframe structural integrity and optimizing aerodynamics), however, this would require a very large force for disconnection (which could damage the rocket and even affect its trajectory off the launch rail). Consequently, this testing campaign helped me make a justified argument for the angle at which both these considerations would be balanced. I then led brainstorm sessions with general members to design a method to ensure the rope for the passive mechanism is kept in line with the QD (to minimize the force needed for disconnection).

I also led the design of a securing mechanism for the quick disconnect within the rocket. This involved selecting appropriate plumbing for the low-pressure system, to ensure a weight, cost, and space-efficient way to secure the QD.
