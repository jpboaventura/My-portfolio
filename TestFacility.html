Design of UAV Flight Test Facility for Flight Path Reconstruction

May. - Jul. 2024 | Prof. Peter R. Grant Vehicle Simulation Laboratory

Description

The project is aimed at developing a flight test facility for a small Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) that can be flown indoors with controlled wind conditions, in order to get precise ground truth data for Flight Path Reconstruction. This project involved the design of an aircraft, the selection & design of sensors, and testing of the equipment.

Role

As an undergraduate summer research student, my responsibility was to find the most optimal trip conditions to ensure that the UAV would behave analogously to a real aircraft. I used XFLR5 software to plot and compare the following relationships for the selected airfoil: lift coefficient (Cl) vs. drag coefficient (Cd), lift coefficient (Cl) vs. angle of attack, and moment coefficient (Cm) vs angle of attack. The UAV was then analyzed for multiple trip conditions using VSPAero software to analyze properties such as the lift coefficient of the selected airfoil throughout the span. Excel spreadsheets were then used to compare the most promising trip conditions. It was found that trip conditions closer to the leading edge produced lower error in the UAV behaviour relative to that of a real aircraft, which is information that can be used when designing the actual UAV. I also selected appropriate sensors, including accelerometers, pitot tubes, gyroscopes, GPS, magnetometers, and barometers, for the collection of required data by the UAV.
